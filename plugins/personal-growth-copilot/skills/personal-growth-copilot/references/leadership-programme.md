## Programme ideas that change practice

| Idea and useful application | Boundary | Source locator in the programme intake |
|---|---|---|
| Three-P contracting: agree professional purpose, practical arrangements, and psychological hopes/concerns. Ask only what matters for this shared work. | Disclosure is voluntary; a conversation cannot grant authority. | `connect-toolkit.md`, p. 6; `accelerate-toolkit.md`, p. 8 |
| Leadership transitions: personal growth to others' growth; specialist to integrator; directive to diplomatic; tactical to strategic. Ask what contribution is now needed and which old responsibilities must move. | Not a ladder of personal worth. Technical judgment and present delivery may remain essential; structural support matters. | `leadership-transitions.md`, PDF pp. 6–10; `accelerate-toolkit.md`, p. 12 |
| Experience → review → conclusions → next attempt. Teach a strategy, let the learner attempt it, examine its effect, and revise the support. | Effort and course completion do not prove capability. Workplace use of sports-coaching ideas is an adaptation. | `self-awareness-toolkit.md`, p. 12; `growth-mindset-dweck.md`, PDF pp. 1–4 |
| Feedback can reveal a difference between intention and impact. Johari offers questions about what self and others know; Action–Impact–Request describes an action, its effect, and a request. | Another person's account is evidence to examine, not a trait verdict. Action–Impact–Request is distinct from CCL's SBI/SBII in L03; do not merge their attribution. | `self-awareness-toolkit.md`, pp. 12–17; feedback slides 39–41 in the source map |
| SCARF names status, certainty, autonomy, relatedness, and fairness as possible self-inquiry prompts. | Ask about the user's experience; never diagnose a colleague's brain state or motive. | `self-awareness-toolkit.md`, p. 7 |
| Read 360 feedback through coverage, group differences, concrete behavior, and context. | Sample reports are illustrative, not the user's results. Do not fabricate/rescore a personal assessment or infer fixed traits. | `self-awareness-toolkit.md`, pp. 20–31 |
| Examine change context: concurrent initiatives, lived culture, pace/scale, history, and kind of change. Consider strain, involvement, coordination, progress, and who repeatedly absorbs the work. | The change-context paper uses interviews/focus-group evidence; change fatigue/addiction is author framing, not a clinical diagnosis or a local causal finding. | `all-change-is-not-equal.md`, PDF pp. 3, 5–13; `change-fatigue-addiction.md`, pp. 2–4 |
| Distinguish missing facts, uncertain effects, and disputed goals. Retrieve, test within authority, or reconcile the decision accordingly. | Context and transition diagrams are prompts, not mandatory emotional stages or permission for uncontrolled experiments. | `accelerate-toolkit.md`, pp. 22–26; library `guides/change-and-influence.md` |
| Restore attention in pressure moments, then examine recovery, workload, coordination, and support. | No universal arousal threshold, clinical protocol, or validated physiology claim is supplied. | `adaptability-toolkit.md`, pp. 12–18; library `guides/pressure-and-energy.md` |

The six library guides connect these ideas to daily work: self and direction,
developing others, communication and trust, change and influence, pressure and energy,
and reflection and lasting contribution. Their portable entry points are the situation
table, team-capability worksheet, rehearsal, and review in
[leadership-practice.md](leadership-practice.md).

The Accelerate synthesis adds a practical test of contribution: what can others
understand, decide, and sustain with the needed knowledge, support, and authority?
A convincing story or immediate enthusiasm does not establish that outcome. Preserve
cultural context; do not reproduce a culturally specific performance as a generic
team-building exercise.

## Latest development and follow-through

The October 12 source changes add or strengthen strategic choice (L12), team capability
and backup coverage, quick-use card openings, and the six-part short practice note.
`templates/role-and-team-review.md` v0.2 and
`templates/quick-practice-note.md` v0.1 are the immediate sources for those portable
worksheets. `HELPER.md` and `MATCHING.md` supply the situation-first selection rules.

The Leading others overview (`leading-others-workshops.md`, pp. 5–14) connects ten
workshop topics to these routes: strategy, people, inclusion, teamwork, wellbeing,
honest conversations, client needs, change, communication, and transitions. It is a
curriculum map, not evidence of attendance or a complete procedure for every named model.

Preserved gaps include ETC's missing procedure, an uncertain handwritten diagram mapping,
missing activity outcomes, and unobserved workplace follow-through. The source map also
retains conflicting Berne/Powell attributions for a communication pyramid; this bundle
does not resolve or teach that diagram as settled fact.

For a future refresh, compare the source README version/date and working changes, then
review relevant cards, HELPER, MATCHING, guides, PROGRAMME-MATERIALS, and templates.
Update this provenance and the affected portable guidance together. Re-run packaging
and reference checks; use real practice observations to propose method changes without
silently treating one case as validation or changing the canonical library.
