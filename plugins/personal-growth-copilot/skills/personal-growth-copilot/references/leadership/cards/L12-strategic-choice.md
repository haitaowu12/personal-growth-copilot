# L12 — Choose a strategic focus

**Job:** choose which longer-term outcome deserves attention and resources when several worthwhile directions compete.

**Record type and relationships:** decision method. Use L11 to clarify uncertain evidence, L09 to resolve workload, L10 to seek cooperation and L08 to test an uncertain effect after a direction is chosen.

**Allow:** 20–30 minutes preparation and 30–45 minutes discussion, followed by a check at an existing decision point; author estimates. Include the decision owner, relevant delivery knowledge and people who would supply or lose capacity.

## Quick use

**When:** several worthwhile directions compete and the choice of outcome is still open.

**Opening:** “Which result matters most over this horizon, and what are we willing to stop or defer to pursue it?”

1. Name the outcome, horizon, decision owner and commitments that must be protected. Retrieve missing strategic direction before assuming it.
2. Compare credible options, including continuing current work, against contribution, evidence, capacity and what each displaces. Keep consequential unknowns visible.
3. Record the choice or recommendation, what stops or waits, the next authorized action and what evidence would reopen the decision.

**Watch:** whether attention and resources actually move, whether the expected result appears, and whose work or service deteriorates. For an overload problem with fixed goals, use L09; for a fixed strategic direction, work on implementation. Read the stop conditions below before acting. Capture an ordinary use in the [short practice note](../practice-note.md).

## Reach for this when

You can describe a result beyond completing the next task, but there are competing ways to contribute. The immediate job is choosing a direction or preparing a recommendation. A list of appealing initiatives is not yet a choice: ask what each would change, what makes that plausible and what it would displace.

## Switch or stop

If the goal and priority are fixed and only the workload is unresolved, use [L09](L09-capacity.md). If stakeholders want incompatible outcomes, use [L04](L04-negotiation.md) or seek an owner decision before comparing implementation options. If one missing fact could change the choice, use [L11](L11-perspective-check.md) to obtain it. Do not use a workshop to assume a business mandate, cancel another owner's commitment or relax required checks. Recommend or defer when you lack decision authority.

## Run it

1. **Frame the choice.** Write the outcome, who benefits, the relevant horizon and why a decision is needed now. Identify the current strategic direction and its source; distinguish a personal ambition from an organizational commitment.
2. **Set the boundary.** Name the decision owner, available resources, mandatory obligations and affected parties. Ask what remains open and what evidence is needed before a choice can be made.
3. **Develop alternatives.** Include continuing the present approach and at least one materially different way to achieve the result. Consider a narrower scope or staged commitment where uncertainty matters. Do not make the preferred option look strong by comparing it only with implausible alternatives.
4. **Compare consequences.** Complete the table below with evidence and assumptions. Ask those carrying the work to correct the effort and opportunity-cost estimates. Explain any difference in the importance of criteria; do not conceal disputed priorities inside a weighted total.
5. **Challenge the preference.** State the strongest objection and the assumption most likely to reverse the choice. Determine whether to retrieve evidence, authorize a bounded test, make a conditional recommendation or decide now. Name the prerequisite owner and evidence if the choice is conditional.
6. **Make the tradeoff explicit.** Record chosen, deferred and rejected options with reasons. State what stops, moves or receives resources, who can authorize those changes and which dependencies need reconciling. A recommendation does not itself change commitments.
7. **Connect direction to action.** Name one next action, its owner and a review point tied to observable evidence. Use [L10](L10-influence.md) for needed cooperation, [L01](L01-delegation.md) for bounded ownership, or [L08](L08-pdsa.md) for an authorized test. Revisit if an important assumption fails or the wider direction changes.

**Output:** a short decision or recommendation: outcome and horizon; alternatives; rationale; authority and conditions; resource tradeoff; next action; evidence and trigger for reconsideration. Keep unknowns and dissent with the decision.

## Fictional example

A practice lead wants more project teams to use systems methods effectively. They compare building another tool, supporting one team with an existing method, and continuing current support. Project interviews identify an immediate gap in applying the existing method, while demand for the new tool remains uncertain. The lead recommends a bounded coaching engagement and deferring tool development. The sponsor still needs to confirm coaching capacity and which planned work can move.

The opening is: “Our intended result is that a team can make and explain this decision using the existing method. What evidence would justify building something new instead?” At an agreed checkpoint, the team would demonstrate its decision and report support effort and unresolved difficulties. This is a fictional recommendation and observation plan; no benefit or resource agreement is claimed.

## Learn from use

Check whether the selected focus changed actual decisions and resource allocation, whether the outcome is becoming observable, and whether displaced work created unexpected costs. Retain reasons for deferral or reversal. A common failure is adding a strategic initiative while keeping every previous commitment. Another is treating an approved plan as evidence of value delivered.

Use the [short practice note](../practice-note.md) for an ordinary decision reflection. For a deliberate test, use the [full practice record](../../behavior-change-experiments.md) to record the prediction and comparator before acting. This card supports one bounded choice; it does not supply a complete enterprise strategy process.

## Test the strategy kernel

Before comparing initiatives, write three connected statements:

1. **Diagnosis:** the obstacle or opportunity that explains why the present approach is insufficient, with supporting evidence and uncertainty.
2. **Guiding policy:** the chosen approach to address that challenge, including what it rules out.
3. **Coherent actions:** the mutually supporting steps, resources and dependencies that put the policy into effect.

Check that the diagnosis explains more than a desired target, the policy addresses that diagnosis, and the actions reinforce one another. Identify any limiting condition that could prevent the actions from producing the intended result. State the observation that would weaken the diagnosis and reopen the choice. If all you have is a goal and an initiative list, return to the challenge before scoring options.
