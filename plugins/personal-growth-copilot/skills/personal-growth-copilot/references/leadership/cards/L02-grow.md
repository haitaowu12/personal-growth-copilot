# L02 — GROW coaching conversation

**Job:** help someone think through an issue and choose a next action they can own.

**Basis:** GROW as described by [Performance Consultants](https://www.performanceconsultants.com/resources/the-grow-model/): Goals, Reality, Options and Will/Way Forward. The source allows movement between stages. The workplace prompts, timebox and example here are original adaptations. Provider descriptions and the [2023 workplace-coaching meta-analysis](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1204166/full) do not establish that this exact script is superior to other coaching approaches.

**Allow:** 5 minutes preparation and 15–25 minutes conversation, followed by an agreed check; author estimates. Two participants are enough.

## Quick use

**When:** someone wants help thinking through a challenge and has meaningful discretion.

**Opening:** “Would coaching, advice or a decision be most useful now?”

1. Agree the goal and examine a concrete recent example.
2. Let the person explore options; check knowledge, authority and capacity.
3. Ask them to choose a next action, support and a review point, or identify what still prevents a choice.

**Watch:** ownership and understanding of the next step, and what happens afterwards. Supply missing instruction directly; resolve overload with L09. Keep fixed decisions and performance instructions explicit.

Read the stop conditions below before acting. ## Reach for this when

The person has a challenge they want to explore and meaningful discretion over what to do. The proposed mechanism is to slow premature advice, expose the actual difficulty and convert reflection into a chosen action.

## Switch or stop

Use direct instruction when someone needs information or a required procedure. Say clearly when a decision is already fixed. Do not conceal a performance warning inside a supposedly open coaching discussion. Resolve capacity with [L09](L09-capacity.md) or decision rights with [L01](L01-delegation.md). This is not a substitute for professional mental-health support.

## Run it

1. Agree the conversation's purpose and ask whether coaching, advice or a decision would help most.
2. **Goal:** “What would make this conversation useful before you return to the work?” Separate today's outcome from a longer-term aspiration.
3. **Reality:** ask for a recent concrete example, what has been tried, what happened and what constraints remain. Distinguish knowledge gaps from assumptions.
4. **Options:** let the person generate alternatives before you add yours. Ask what a small version or a request for help would look like. Advice can be offered openly, with permission.
5. **Way forward:** ask them to select an action, identify support and choose evidence and timing for review. If they cannot choose, explore the unresolved obstacle rather than demanding commitment.
6. Check the action against authority, quality and workload boundaries. Summarize who will do what.

**Output:** the person's chosen next step, reason, support and review point. A completed acronym is not the output.

## Fictional example

A technical specialist feels unheard in steering meetings. Their goal is to make one decision request clearer. Reviewing the last meeting reveals that the decision was buried in background detail. They consider a one-page brief, an advance conversation and a shorter opening. They choose to test a two-minute outcome/options/request opening and ask a colleague what decision they understood. This is a proposed experiment, not proof of increased influence.

## Learn from use

Observe whether the person can explain and carry out the next action. Ask whether they experienced the choice as theirs. Track hidden coaching workload and missed instruction. A common failure is asking leading questions until the person guesses the manager's preferred answer. See the [practice record](../../behavior-change-experiments.md).

## Use a short coaching conversation

For a brief everyday exchange, select the questions needed to move through GROW rather than forcing a long form. Ask what matters now, invite another possibility, identify the real challenge for the person, clarify the result they want and the help they seek, then agree a next action. Close by asking which part was useful. Ask one question at a time and wait for the answer.

If you notice yourself preparing the solution before understanding the challenge, use that as the trigger to ask one more genuine question. Coaching should not withhold an answer, instruction or resource the person actually needs. A request for help can be accepted, declined or negotiated; it need not transfer ownership of the whole task.

## Use a one-to-one to find the help actually needed

Invite the person's agenda: current work, emerging concerns, decisions needed and something they want to learn. Hear their account before selecting coaching, instruction, resources or an operational decision. End with each person's next action and a proportionate check. A one-to-one can include coaching without making every issue a coaching problem.

Match support to evidence of readiness for **this task**. Someone experienced in one context may need demonstration and supported practice in another. Agree the decision boundary and monitoring; reduce or increase support as the task and evidence change. For team coverage, record this in the [capability worksheet](../../leadership-practice.md#team-capability-and-continuity#team-capability-and-continuity).
