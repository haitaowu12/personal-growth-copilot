# L06 — Brief and huddle

**Job:** establish a shared plan before work and update it when conditions change.

**Basis:** adaptation of AHRQ TeamSTEPPS [brief](https://www.ahrq.gov/teamstepps-program/curriculum/team/tools/briefs.html) and [huddle](https://www.ahrq.gov/teamstepps-program/curriculum/team/tools/huddle.html) guidance. The primary context is healthcare; engineering roles and the example below are our translation. This is communication support, not an operating procedure or permission to perform technical work.

**Allow:** 5 minutes preparation, a 5–10 minute brief or 3–5 minute huddle, and confirmation of changes; author estimates. Include the people doing the work and those who own consequential dependencies.

## Quick use

**When:** a team needs a shared plan or a reset after circumstances change.

**Opening:** “Here is the outcome and what has changed. What does that mean for each person's next action?”

1. Confirm the goal, accountable owner, current evidence, roles and limits.
2. Identify changes to sequence, commitments, resources and dependent teams; obtain the relevant owner's disposition of superseded work.
3. Ask affected people to explain their next action and raise conflicts; record decisions and the next check.

**Watch:** shared understanding and whether obsolete instructions still drive work. Reconcile conflicting authority before presenting a settled plan; operate within the applicable incident procedure when urgent action is required.

## Reach for this when

People are about to act on a shared task or their assumptions no longer agree. A brief occurs before work. A huddle revisits the plan during work. The proposed mechanism is to make roles, resources, changes and contingencies explicit before coordination fails.

## Switch or stop

Do not use a huddle to bypass an approval, required technical review or incident command. If you need to generate alternatives rather than align execution, use [L05](L05-participation.md). If the event has finished and the question is what to learn, use [L07](L07-debrief.md).

## Run it

1. State the immediate outcome and the accountable leader for this activity.
2. Confirm roles, interfaces and the current plan. Invite each participant to identify a missing resource, dependency or conflicting assumption.
3. Name constraints and the conditions that require a stop, escalation or revised plan.
4. Ask people responsible for critical actions to state their understanding. Silence is not a confirmation.
5. During a huddle, start with what changed. Decide whether to retain, adapt or suspend the plan within existing authority.
6. Record consequential changes, who must know, and who may authorize resumption. Confirm that absent affected parties receive the update.

**Output:** a concise shared plan: purpose, roles, sequence, dependencies, limits and next check. Retain only material decisions, not a transcript of every briefing.

## Fictional example

A document-review team starts a two-hour workshop. The chair confirms that the goal is to resolve disputed comments, not approve the design. Midway, participants discover they are using different revisions. A huddle identifies the agreed review copy, suspends dispositions affected by the mismatch and assigns reconciliation. The plan changes; no design acceptance is implied.

## Learn from use

Check whether participants can explain the plan and whether later surprises concerned something the brief could reasonably have surfaced. Also measure meeting burden and unnecessary interruption. A common misuse is turning a huddle into a long status-reporting meeting. Use the [practice record](../../behavior-change-experiments.md).

## Reset the goal and reconnect the team

- State the old goal, new goal, reason and decision authority. Identifying an obsolete goal does not authorize cancelling its commitments; check dependent teams and the actual cancellation owner first. Identify what work or instruction is superseded and what remains valid.
- Ask affected people what the change means for their next action, dependencies and capacity. Treat conflicting answers as useful evidence of an incomplete brief.
- Confirm how absent participants receive and acknowledge material changes. A sent message is not evidence of shared understanding.
- If two authorized directions still conflict, seek reconciliation from the relevant owners. Do not ask the team to absorb incompatible demands through a more positive attitude.

**Words to try:** “Previously we were aiming for ___. The authorized change is ___. Stop or pause ___; continue ___. What does this change for your next step, and what does it make impossible?” This is our programme-informed extension of the AHRQ adaptation.

## Check active readiness

After explaining the plan, invite each participant to state their role, critical dependency and response to one plausible change. Resolve gaps and have the authorized owner determine whether the work is ready to proceed. Hearing a briefing is not itself evidence of understanding.

**Source addition:** Marquet’s “Don’t brief, certify” mechanism (competence chapter note (source locator: research/leadership-toolbox/books/turn-the-ship-around/compilation/chapters/03-competence.md), E004; PDF pp.138–140). Here it is adapted as an active readiness check within the existing brief. It does not cancel a required briefing, replace a prescribed assurance process or award a formal qualification.
