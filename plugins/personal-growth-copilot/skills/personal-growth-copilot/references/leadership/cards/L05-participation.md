# L05 — 1-2-4-All

**Job:** make room for individual thinking and small-group development before a whole-group discussion.

**Basis:** the [Liberating Structures authors' instructions](https://www.liberatingstructures.com/1-1-2-4-all/), read September 28, 2026. The current page describes roughly 15 minutes and a sequence of individual, pair, quartet and whole-group work. Its claims about inclusion and ownership are method-author claims; this research pass did not establish a causal effect for this structure in technical leadership. The decision record below is an added step.

**Allow:** 5–10 minutes preparation and about 15–20 minutes activity, plus actual decision time. At least four participants; the source gives four as the minimum. Adjust timing explicitly for language, accessibility and remote conditions.

## Quick use

**When:** a group needs ideas beyond the first or loudest voices and people can contribute freely.

**Opening:** “The question is ___. Your input will inform ___, and the decision belongs to ___.”

1. State the question and decision boundary, then allow silent individual thinking.
2. Develop ideas in pairs, then quartets, preserving distinct views and accessible participation.
3. Share selected insights with the whole group; retain objections and identify how input will reach the decision owner.

**Watch:** which ideas affect the decision and whether people felt able to disagree. This summarizes the sequence; use the full timing guidance below. Do not run a public exercise where challenge risks retaliation.

Read the stop conditions below before acting. ## Reach for this when

A group needs multiple ideas and normal plenary discussion favours the first or loudest speakers. Individual reflection before discussion can reduce immediate dependence on the leader's opening answer. This creates opportunities to speak; it cannot by itself establish psychological safety.

## Switch or stop

Do not imply participants can decide when they are only advising. If challenge risks retaliation, change the conditions before using a visible group exercise. Do not delay urgent required action to run an idea workshop. For a known plan needing coordination use [L06](L06-brief-huddle.md).

## Run it

1. State one clear challenge, the decision boundary and how ideas will be used. Do not announce your preferred answer first unless an essential constraint requires it.
2. Give silent individual thinking/writing time. The current source allocates one minute after the introduction.
3. Move into pairs to compare and develop ideas; the source allocates two minutes.
4. Combine pairs into quartets; the current source allocates five minutes. Preserve distinct ideas and disagreements instead of forcing consensus.
5. Return to the group for selected insights; the current source allocates seven minutes. Avoid repeating every conversation verbatim.
6. **Local extension:** retain a short option list, material objections, evidence gaps and the next decision owner. Explain which input changed the decision, or why it was not adopted.

## Fictional example

Eight discipline leads are asked how to reduce repeated review reopenings without overlooking new evidence. They think individually, discuss in pairs and combine into two groups of four. One option is a changed-scope summary; another is clearer closure evidence. A minority concern about stale assumptions is retained. The chair commissions a small test of the summary and names an owner to resolve the concern. The exercise has generated options, not proved consensus or improvement.

## Learn from use

Observe which ideas survive into decisions and ask whether participants felt able to disagree. More speaking turns alone is insufficient. A common failure is compressing silent time and letting the leader filter every group's output. Use the [practice record](../../behavior-change-experiments.md).

## Connect participation to a decision

Before inviting contributions, say what question is open, which evidence is needed, who will decide and how people will hear the rationale. After generating ideas, compare the evidence and objections through that decision route. Preserve a dissenting view even if it does not change the decision. Participation does not imply consensus.

## Respond so that people can speak up again

Before the exercise, explain why the work needs information you may not hold and which constraints remain fixed. Ask for concerns before announcing your preferred solution. Offer a separate channel when public contribution is inappropriate.

After someone raises an issue, acknowledge the contribution, clarify the substance and explain what will happen next. “Thank you for raising that. What did you observe, and what do we need to check?” is a local opening. Appreciation for a report does not endorse every claim or proposal. Close the loop even when the idea is not adopted, and address clear conduct violations through the proper process.

Observe whether concerns still surface after a difficult response. More speaking turns, agreement or a successful workshop cannot by themselves establish psychological safety. **Source:** Edmondson, *The Fearless Organization* (catalog 2018; copyright 2019), chapters 1 and 7; source notes (source locator: research/leadership-toolbox/books/the-fearless-organization/compilation/BOOK_OVERVIEW.md), E001–E004.
