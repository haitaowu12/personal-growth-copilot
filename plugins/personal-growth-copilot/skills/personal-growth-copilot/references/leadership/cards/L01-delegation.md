# L01 — Outcome delegation agreement

**Job:** transfer a bounded piece of work with enough decision authority, capability and support for its owner to deliver.

**Basis:** local synthesis, not a branded or empirically validated protocol. It extends the goal/role/resource clarification in [AHRQ's brief](https://www.ahrq.gov/teamstepps-program/curriculum/team/tools/briefs.html) into an ongoing work agreement. Decision boundaries, checking and the prompts below are our adaptation. Healthcare guidance supports the source description; it does not establish effects in engineering delegation.

**Allow:** 10 minutes preparation, 20–30 minutes together and a checkpoint matched to the work; author estimates. Participants: delegating leader, proposed owner and any necessary resource/approval owner.

## Quick use

**When:** a bounded responsibility can move, but ownership or decision rights need agreement.

**Opening:** “What could you own here, and what decisions and support would make that workable?”

1. Check the outcome, task knowledge, capacity and authority with the prospective owner.
2. Agree independent decisions, required checks, support, acceptance evidence and escalation.
3. Set a checkpoint and recovery path; state what the leader stops doing and what ownership remains if intervention is needed.

**Watch:** the owner's judgment and experience, quality, available support and hidden rework. Resolve missing capacity with L09; provide instruction or supervision for a knowledge gap. Fewer questions alone do not show success.

Read the stop conditions below before acting. ## Reach for this when

Work repeatedly returns to a leader because the recipient cannot tell what they may decide or what a satisfactory result looks like. The proposed mechanism is to remove ambiguity while making support and accountability explicit. Check the recipient's account; repeated requests for help may instead reflect overload or missing expertise.

## Switch or stop

Do not delegate authority you do not hold. Keep required independent checks and approvals. If capability is missing, teach, pair or narrow the assignment. If capacity is missing, use [L09](L09-capacity.md). If rights are clear but the owner needs to develop options, use [L02](L02-grow.md).

## Run it

1. Explain the useful outcome and recipient of the work, not just the document to produce.
2. Ask the prospective owner to restate the result, identify unknowns and check available capacity.
3. Agree what they decide independently, what needs consultation and what requires approval. Name the approver rather than writing “management.”
4. Set acceptance evidence, non-negotiable constraints, resources and an escalation trigger. Distinguish a mandatory check from your preferred working style.
5. Let the owner propose an approach. If you prescribe part of it, explain the constraint that requires it.
6. Agree the first evidence-bearing checkpoint and what support you will provide. Ask: “What could make you unable to deliver within this boundary?”
7. At the checkpoint, ask for the owner's recommendation and reasoning. Take work back only for a stated reason; record what support or boundary failed.

**Output:** a short agreement: outcome; owner; independent decisions; consult/approve decisions; quality evidence; resources; checkpoint; escalation conditions.

## Fictional example

A systems lead keeps rewriting an analyst's interface summary. They agree that the analyst owns the summary and can choose its presentation; changes to interface requirements remain outside that authority. The analyst must show source references and unresolved conflicts, with an independent technical check before issue. The first checkpoint reviews two sample interfaces. The lead says: “Bring your preferred treatment of the conflicting records and why; I will help resolve access and authority gaps.” The output is a clear work agreement, not a claim of improved performance.

## Learn from use

Check whether the owner can explain their decisions, whether required quality is met and how much avoidable takeover occurred. Also ask whether support was actually available. Less leader involvement with more hidden rework is not success. A common failure is nominal delegation while the leader changes every discretionary choice. Use the [practice record](../../behavior-change-experiments.md).

## Programme application and recovery

Before transfer, identify what the leader stops doing and what support time both parties need. Agree a recovery path: if capacity, evidence quality or a dependency fails, pause, narrow or replan the assignment with its owner. A temporary takeover should state its reason, the decisions the owner retains and when ownership will be reviewed. Avoid quietly rewriting the work and leaving responsibility nominally with the other person.

**Counterexample:** a capable colleague agrees enthusiastically but has no available capacity. Do not interpret agreement as feasibility; resolve workload with L09 first. At the next checkpoint, review their account of ownership alongside quality and hidden effort. Author extension added October 3; no outcome claimed.

A learner with a bounded task and adequate assessed supervision may be a suitable owner. Check protected learning time and support availability before proceeding. Evaluate growing judgment and appropriate escalation; fewer requests for help alone may indicate withdrawal or silence.

## Invite intent and retain support

Once the scope is agreed, ask the proposed owner to describe the action they intend to take, their reasons, relevant checks and where they would escalate. Confirm three conditions separately: the decision they may make, competence for that decision, and understanding of the purpose and constraints. If one is missing, narrow the decision or provide support before expanding discretion.

When help is requested, clarify the gap and contribute what is needed, then explicitly return the next action to its owner. A useful opening is: “You still own this outcome. What support would help you take the next step?” Do not convert support into silent takeover or withdrawal.

## Check whether helping has become taking over

Use the revised *Multipliers* when you intend to help but find that others wait for your answer or hand work back. Choose one recent interaction and ask what your intervention enabled or displaced. Try a smaller response: clarify the obstacle, ask what help is wanted, provide that support and return the next action to the agreed owner.

Keep instruction, resources and checks where they are needed. Doing less is not an excuse to leave someone unsupported.
