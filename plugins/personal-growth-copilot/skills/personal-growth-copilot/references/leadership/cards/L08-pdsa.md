# L08 — PDSA change experiment

**Job:** learn whether a specific change helps in the actual setting before expanding it.

**Basis:** [Institute for Healthcare Improvement, Model for Improvement: Testing Changes](https://www.ihi.org/library/model-for-improvement/testing-changes). IHI describes linked Plan–Do–Study–Act cycles, explicit predictions, observations and revisions. The source establishes the method; our leadership application, planning allowance and example are adaptations. A [2019 systematic review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12913-019-4482-6) found that just 3 of 72 sufficiently documented projects met all four methodological features it assessed. This concerns reporting and fidelity in healthcare QI projects, not a failure probability for this card. Calling an activity PDSA does not establish that it was implemented well or caused improvement.

**Allow:** 20–30 minutes to design a small test, one bounded occurrence or work cycle, and 15–30 minutes to review; author estimates. Include affected participants and anyone who must authorize the change.

## Quick use

**When:** an uncertain change can be tested within an authorized, recoverable boundary.

**Opening:** “If we try ___, what do we expect to observe, and what would make us stop?”

1. Plan the prediction, comparator, smallest informative test, observations and stop conditions.
2. Do the authorized test and record actual actions, deviations and effects.
3. Study the evidence against the prediction, then act: adapt, retest, adopt locally or abandon with a reason.

**Watch:** the intended result alongside quality, effort and work shifted to others. Use the full practice record before a deliberate test. Mandatory controls and missing authority cannot be bypassed by calling an action an experiment.

Read the stop conditions below before acting. ## Reach for this when

There is a plausible change, an outcome you can observe, permission to test and an acceptable way to stop or recover. This supports a change strategy by generating local evidence. It does not replace deciding strategic objectives or addressing conflicting interests.

## Switch or stop

Do not experiment with mandatory safety controls or protected operations without their authorized process. A vague problem needs inquiry before a test. If there is no capacity, resolve [L09](L09-capacity.md) first. If parties disagree on the outcome or costs, [L04](L04-negotiation.md) may be needed.

## Run it

1. Define the desired result and the problem in observable terms. Identify the current comparison or starting evidence.
2. **Plan:** write a prediction, the smallest informative test, participants, timing, observations and stop conditions. Add a balancing measure so faster work does not conceal poorer quality or shifted workload.
3. **Do:** run the authorized test and record what actually occurred, including deviations and unexpected effects.
4. **Study:** compare observations with the prediction. Distinguish a completed action, participant reaction and downstream result. Consider plausible alternative explanations.
5. **Act:** adopt locally, adapt, retest or abandon. State why and what the next cycle needs to learn. One favourable attempt does not justify broad rollout.

**Output:** a one-page experiment record with prediction, action, evidence and next decision. Use the [practice record](../../behavior-change-experiments.md).

## Fictional example

A team tests a short decision brief before one recurring meeting. The prediction is that attendees will identify the requested decision without a long oral reconstruction. They record whether the decision was understood, preparation time and any evidence omitted. If clarity improves but preparation doubles, they simplify the brief before trying it again. No fictional numerical result is presented as observed evidence.

## Learn from use

Check whether the prediction could actually have been wrong, whether observations were collected and whether the next cycle changed because of the evidence. A common failure is doing a change, declaring success and adding the PDSA label afterwards. Local learning is valuable even without a causal estimate; keep that limitation explicit.
