# Capture one use

For an ordinary conversation or decision, copy the six prompts into a private working note and give a retained record its own ID. Use only the detail needed to learn. This is a short version of the [experiment design](../behavior-change-experiments.md); use that fuller record for a deliberate test or method comparison. Keep learning notes separate from personnel appraisal.

## Before

1. **Situation and aim:** date, what happened, whose account this is, and the result sought. Note a recent comparison if available; otherwise the first observation is a baseline.
2. **Response and boundary:** card/version or approach, why it fits, what you expect, who decides and what is actually agreed. Record any missing authority, capacity, support or participation condition, and what would make you stop or switch.

## After

3. **What happened:** attempted, declined, deferred or abandoned; what you actually did and what was agreed, disputed or left open.
4. **What changed:** concrete observation, date and evidence. Separate the immediate output from a later result; mark the later result unknown until observed. If writing retrospectively, label any reconstructed expectation rather than presenting it as a prior prediction.
5. **Other account and cost:** the affected person's feedback or its absence, required quality, support received, extra effort or unintended effects. Leave unavailable feedback unknown.
6. **Next:** keep, adapt, switch or stop, with a reason; the next existing checkpoint if agreed, and any proposed change to the card.

A declined attempt can still teach something. One favourable experience does not establish general effectiveness. Link this note from the relevant private project record; add a reusable lesson to the existing card only when the evidence supports it.
