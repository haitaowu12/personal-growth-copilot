# Evidence and Decision Readiness

Use this reference when copied messages matter, accounts conflict, emotion is
high, or the next action is difficult to reverse.

## Separate the evidence classes

| Class | Meaning | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Record | Visible wording, timestamp, author, audience, or version history | Quote or summarize precisely |
| Observation | What the user directly saw or heard | Attribute to the user |
| Second-hand report | What another person said occurred | Preserve the source and uncertainty |
| Interpretation | A meaning assigned to the evidence | Treat as a hypothesis |
| Unknown | Missing information that may change the decision | Ask only if decision-changing |
| Contradiction | Two claims that cannot both be accepted as stated | Preserve; do not force resolution |
| Stale fact | Previously true information that may no longer apply | Verify before relying on it |

Do not upgrade a screenshot, silence, delayed reply, facial expression, or
polite phrase into proof of intent.

## Build the minimum case record

Capture only what affects the decision:

- who communicated with whom;
- exact or approximate timing;
- channel and audience;
- the specific behavior or wording;
- prior relevant attempts;
- concrete impact;
- current decision and deadline.

Use roles or aliases. Exclude direct identifiers, unrelated history, gossip,
health information, account details, and private data that do not change the
recommended action.

## Test each interpretation

For every material reading, ask:

1. What evidence supports it?
2. What evidence does not fit it?
3. What is the strongest plausible alternative?
4. What observable event would strengthen or weaken it?
5. Does the recommended action remain sensible if the interpretation is wrong?

Prefer actions that are useful across several plausible readings.

## Check readiness

Emotion can signal importance, violation, uncertainty, or accumulated strain.
It does not prove a theory about another person.

Before an irreversible act, check:

- Is the user asking for immediate action while flooded, exhausted, or afraid?
- Is there a deadline, or only a felt need to respond?
- Can the message remain unsent for a short review?
- Would preserving the record or contacting one trusted person reduce risk?
- Is the user able to carry out the stated consequence?

Use the smallest stabilizing step that preserves agency. Do not impose a long
therapeutic preface on a composed user.

## Ask only decision-changing questions

A question is useful only if its answer can change:

- whether to act;
- the intervention level;
- directness;
- channel;
- timing;
- escalation;
- safety planning;
- stopping condition.

Otherwise state the assumption and proceed.

## Produce an evidence boundary

When ambiguity matters, include a compact boundary such as:

> The record supports X. It does not establish Y. A plausible alternative is
> Z. The next step is designed to work under either reading.
