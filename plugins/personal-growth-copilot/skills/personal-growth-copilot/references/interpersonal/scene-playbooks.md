# Interpersonal Scene Playbooks

Load this reference only after selecting the leading mechanism. Use one
playbook, add a material overlay when needed, and keep the final answer lighter
than this source.

Every playbook contains a trigger, preflight, action, starter, branches, and a
stop or escalation rule. Apply the power, safety, multilingual, AI, or
multi-actor overlay whenever its trigger is present.

## 1. Managing-up disagreement

**Trigger:** The user disagrees with a manager's direction, estimate, priority,
or technical decision.

**Preflight:** Decision owner, duty to advise or comply, evidence, mandatory
obligation, audience, deadline, retaliation exposure, and record.

**Action:** State the shared outcome; present the bounded concern and evidence;
compare options against criteria; make one recommendation; ask for the decision;
record owner, assumptions, dissent, and warning point.

**Starter:**

> I want to support Friday's decision. The current option creates ___ risk
> because ___. My recommendation is A. If you choose B, I need the exception and
> decision owner recorded before implementation.

**Branches:** If heard, confirm. If directed otherwise, distinguish ordinary
disagreement from a mandatory concern. If authority is unclear, identify the
real decider. If the concern is omitted from the record, use documented or
supported voice.

**Stop/escalate:** Do not relitigate an ordinary authorized decision. Use the
qualified route when a mandatory safety, legal, ethical, or governance concern
remains unresolved.

## 2. Workload and incompatible priorities

**Trigger:** A new request conflicts with current commitments.

**Preflight:** Existing owners, dates, definition of done, capacity, verification
requirements, cost of acceptance, and whether the request is genuinely optional.

**Action:** Show current commitments; offer two feasible packages; state what
each displaces; ask the decision owner to prioritize; document the choice and
warning point.

**Starter:**

> I can deliver A by Thursday, or move to B today and shift A to Monday. Doing
> both by Thursday would remove the verification step. Which outcome should take
> priority?

**Branches:** If told “both,” state required resource or accepted risk. If no
decision arrives, continue the last authorized priority when safe and state the
assumption. If repeated, request a standing intake rule.

**Stop/escalate:** Do not silently absorb undefined work. Escalate material
safety, quality, contractual, or livelihood exposure through the proper owner.

## 3. Feedback and accountability

**Trigger:** A specific behavior or recurring miss needs to change.

**Preflight:** Purpose, evidence, controllability, standard, authority, support,
forum, public image cost, and whether the problem is structural.

**Action:** Name behavior and impact; invite material context; state the
standard; agree support, owner, date, and warning point; schedule verification;
recap neutrally.

**Starter:**

> The last three reports contained errors in the totals, delaying release review.
> What is contributing to the pattern? The required standard is a verified total
> before handoff. Let's agree the check, support, warning point, and review date.

**Branches:** Repair unclear instruction or resources when present. Clarify
authority if the standard is disputed. Use the pre-agreed accountability route
if the commitment fails.

**Stop/escalate:** Do not demand a character admission. Move to a qualified
performance process only when evidence, authority, prior support, and current
policy justify it.

## 4. Credit and attribution correction

**Trigger:** Work was omitted, blurred, or attributed inaccurately.

**Preflight:** Ordinary contribution records, all legitimate contributors,
public record needing correction, decision audience, and retaliation exposure.

**Action:** Correct privately when safe; describe contributions factually;
request a concrete record update; agree future presentation and attribution;
surface a repeated documented pattern to the appropriate owner.

**Starter:**

> In today's meeting, the analysis was described as your model. I built the
> model, and you incorporated it into the deck. Please correct the recap to name
> both contributions. Before the next meeting, let's agree how each section is
> presented and credited.

**Branches:** If corrected, confirm the record. If intent is debated, return to
observable contribution. Correct other omissions too. If private correction is
unsafe, use supported or documented voice.

**Stop/escalate:** No mass accusation, secret coalition, or motive verdict.
Escalate the factual pattern and decision impact.

## 5. Exclusion from access or a decision forum

**Trigger:** Decisions affecting the user occur in a forum they cannot access.

**Preflight:** Forum, real decider, invitation owner, unique information,
affected constituency, proxy adequacy, implementer, verifier, and needed access
level.

**Action:** Bound the decision; identify the information or impact requiring
representation; ask for the smallest useful change—agenda slot, written input,
proxy with a mandate, verifier contact, record, or review right; observe whether
decisions change.

**Starter:**

> This forum is deciding ___, and my team holds ___ information needed for that
> decision. Please add me for that item. If attendance is not workable, I need
> the decision question in advance, a route to submit and verify our evidence,
> and the decision record afterward.

**Branches:** If it is the wrong forum, find the real path. If a proxy lacks the
information or authority, create direct input. If exclusion repeats, involve the
decision or governance owner with factual impact.

**Stop/escalate:** Do not infer sabotage or score influence. Escalate when a
required information source, verifier, implementer, or affected voice remains
structurally absent.

## 6. Sponsorship, networking, and advocacy

**Trigger:** The user wants access, an introduction, visibility, mentoring, or
promotion advocacy.

**Preflight:** Relationship evidence, relevance, request size, effort, timing,
discretion, reciprocity expectations, and a real decline path.

**Action:** Ask for readiness feedback; close evidence gaps; request one bounded
exposure or introduction; follow through; request advocacy only when the person
can support it honestly.

**Starter:**

> You have seen my work on A and B. I am preparing for roles that require C.
> Would you be willing to identify what evidence is still missing? If the fit is
> there later, I may ask whether you can introduce me to the program lead.

**Branches:** If they cannot help, ask for direction rather than pressure. If a
gap appears, create evidence. If declined, preserve the relationship without
repeated asks.

**Stop/escalate:** No flattery debt, manufactured intimacy, implied obligation,
or pressure after refusal.

## 7. Conflict containment and re-entry

**Trigger:** A disagreement has become repetitive, personal, heated, or unsafe.

**Preflight:** Task, process, role, relationship, values, and safety lanes; heat;
audience; power; dependency; and whether the goal is solution, containment,
coexistence, repair, or exit.

**Action:** Pause with a specific re-entry plan; separate issue lanes; choose one
decision question and criteria; assign owner, participants, timebox, and record;
re-enter or contain; mediate, escalate, or disengage if the process fails.

**Starter:**

> We have moved from the design question into claims about competence. Let's
> pause. Tomorrow we will compare the options against the approved criteria,
> with ___ deciding, and record the unresolved risks.

**Branches:** If both can re-enter, proceed. If one refuses structure, use
authority or mediation. For enduring values conflict, define coexistence or
non-interference. For threat or retaliation, move to protection.

**Stop/escalate:** No forced dialogue in unsafe cases and no repeated
unstructured “clear the air” sessions.

## 8. Repair after a concrete breach

**Trigger:** A verified miss, disclosure, concealment, or other breach damaged
reliance.

**Preflight:** Accepted facts, disputed intent, impact, severity, recurrence,
remedy, authority, exposure, and whether renewed reliance is necessary.

**Action:** Acknowledge behavior and impact; accept supported responsibility;
restore the loss; change controls; define verification behavior; select no
reliance, minimum access, dual control, a small trial, or normal reliance after
evidence.

**Starter:**

> I sent the wrong file and exposed the draft. That was my error. I have removed
> access, notified the authorized owner, and added a two-person release check.
> Future access should depend on demonstrated control, not this message.

**Branches:** Do not confess disputed intent. Involve the correct authority when
the breach cannot be repaired privately. Respect requested distance. Use minimum
necessary access when exposure remains material.

**Stop/escalate:** Do not use apology to secure forgiveness, silence, or a waiver
of process. A re-entry trial is not mandatory.

## 9. Refusal and recurring boundary enforcement

**Trigger:** A request exceeds capacity, consent, privacy, money, time, or an
agreed boundary.

**Preflight:** User control, necessary disclosure, genuine alternative, power,
exposure, and feasible consequence.

**Action:** State the decision; give minimum context; offer an alternative only
if genuine; repeat once without new justification; execute the user-controlled
consequence.

**Starter:**

> I can't lend more money while the previous loan is unpaid. I won't discuss a
> new loan. We can agree on repayment of the existing amount.

**Branches:** If accepted, move on. If challenged, repeat once. If pressure
continues, end the exchange or use support.

**Stop/escalate:** A boundary is not an opening bid. Stop after the selected
repeat branch; route coercion or threat.

## 10. Asking for help, access, or a favor

**Trigger:** The user needs assistance without vague obligation.

**Preflight:** Purpose, exact effort, timing, relevance, alternative, information
sensitivity, reciprocity boundary, and decline path.

**Action:** State purpose; make a bounded request; specify effort and timing;
make declining real; close the loop and credit help.

**Starter:**

> Could you review the two-page risk summary for technical gaps by Thursday? I
> expect about 20 minutes. If this week is full, no problem—I can ask the
> assurance team.

**Branches:** Confirm scope on acceptance. Decide explicitly on a counteroffer.
Use the alternative after a decline.

**Stop/escalate:** Do not turn status, friendship, or past help into debt.

## 11. Friend, family, roommate, or neighbor pattern

**Trigger:** A repeated everyday or relationship behavior affects shared money,
space, time, care, commitments, privacy, or respect.

**Preflight:** Relationship norm—communal care, shared arrangement, voluntary
connection, or mixed norm—plus event, pattern, known constraint, dependency,
housing/financial exposure, and safety.

**Action:** Name the norm and behavior; distinguish temporary need from a stable
pattern; state practical impact; renegotiate one sustainable arrangement; set a
review window; implement a user-controlled consequence only when a legitimate
agreement fails.

**Starter:**

> We agreed to alternate kitchen cleaning, and the last three turns were missed.
> I need the agreed turn completed by Sunday. If that does not happen over the
> next two weeks, I will stop sharing this arrangement and we will need a
> different allocation.

**Branches:** If the norm or agreement was unclear, clarify it. If illness or
care need creates temporary asymmetry, discuss capacity rather than immediate
reciprocity. If a shared arrangement keeps failing, implement the consequence.

**Stop/escalate:** Do not diagnose, parent, shame, count every favor, or withdraw
as a secret test. Route housing, legal, or safety questions appropriately.

## 12. Formal-route preparation without adjudication

**Trigger:** The user may need HR, union, ombuds, regulator, legal,
safeguarding, or another qualified process.

**Preflight:** Immediate safety, jurisdiction, current policy, deadlines,
retaliation exposure, evidence, desired interim protection, and available
authoritative information.

**Action:** Build a minimal chronology; separate source classes; list impact,
witnesses, prior attempts, and preservation status; define questions and desired
process outcomes; verify the current route; avoid declaring the category.

**Starter:**

> I want guidance on the applicable process. I have organized the dates,
> behaviors, records, witnesses, impact, and prior attempts. My immediate
> questions are ___, and the interim protection I am requesting is ___.

**Branches:** Prioritize urgent protection. Find the correct authority when the
first route lacks it. Verify deadlines and current rules with an official source
or qualified professional.

**Stop/escalate:** Do not investigate covertly, coach testimony, expose
unnecessary private material, or state a legal or HR conclusion.

## 13. Digital or text ambiguity

**Trigger:** Tone, delay, punctuation, read receipts, short replies, or channel
behavior is being treated as a social verdict.

**Preflight:** Exact cue, established local norm, urgency, time zone, missing
context, repetition, and operational decision.

**Action:** Classify signal strength; identify the decision hidden behind the
tone question; ask one content-focused clarification or move channels for a
specific reason; negotiate norms if recurring; update from follow-through.

**Starter:**

> I may be reading too much into the short reply. I need the decision rather
> than a tone interpretation: should I proceed with A, or hold until we speak
> tomorrow?

**Branches:** Stop tone analysis after clarification. Address repeated avoidance
around decisions as a pattern. Use a bounded call plus recap when text fails.

**Stop/escalate:** No repeated message tests, activity monitoring, or hostility
inference from latency alone.

## 14. Cross-cultural or mixed-language mismatch

**Trigger:** Directness, silence, disagreement, hierarchy, face, proficiency, or
channel may affect the interaction.

**Preflight:** Speech act, roles, public/private setting, imposition, local team
norms, language proficiency, individual history, record need, and audience.

**Action:** Describe the observed mismatch without national explanation; state
the function required; offer two participation or wording formats; ask the
person's preference; confirm meaning, authority, boundary, and date.

**Starter:**

> In the meeting I did not hear a clear agree/disagree response. For this
> decision, I need any technical concern before Friday. Would you prefer to
> discuss it privately or send written comments?

**Branches:** Simplify and confirm meaning when proficiency matters. Create a
safer route when hierarchy affects the forum. Update from individual evidence,
not cultural labels.

**Stop/escalate:** No national personality inference, compulsory indirectness,
or cultural excuse for a documented breach.

## 15. Multi-actor decision and information topology

**Trigger:** More than two actors influence information, authority, impact,
implementation, or ratification.

**Preflight:** Forum, recommender, decider, veto, funder, implementer, verifier,
ratifier, shared and unique information, affected constituencies, proxy limits,
and sensitive judgments.

**Action:** Bound the decision; map FAICR; elicit uniquely held evidence before
convergence; protect high-exposure contributors; identify the smallest actor or
evidence addition; obtain ratification; close the loop with a decision record.

**Starter:**

> Before choosing a message, we need the decision path. ___ owns the decision,
> ___ holds unique evidence, ___ implements the result, ___ carries the impact,
> and ___ must ratify it. The smallest useful move is to bring ___ evidence into
> ___ forum before the decision date.

**Branches:** Clarify governance when ownership is unclear. Replace an
inadequate proxy with direct or independently verified input. Keep sensitive
influence hypotheses private and action-focused.

**Stop/escalate:** No manufactured alliance, gossip, opposition score, or
assumption that low power means low importance.

## 16. Rebalancing invisible or low-promotability work

**Trigger:** The user repeatedly receives necessary work with limited visibility
or development value.

**Preflight:** Frequency, time, opportunity cost, allocation method,
beneficiaries, formal role, decision-maker, visibility, and retaliation risk.

**Action:** Make work and displaced cost visible; propose rotation, scope
reduction, resourcing, recognition, or developmental pairing; ask for an
allocation rule; record the next allocation and review window.

**Starter:**

> I have handled notes and follow-up for six of the last seven meetings. I can
> cover this week, but after that I need the role rotated or connected to
> ownership of the workstream. Can we agree the allocation rule?

**Branches:** If accepted, record it. If dismissed as “just helping,” state time
and displaced deliverable. Lower exposure and seek qualified advice when a
protected-status pattern may matter without making a formal finding.

**Stop/escalate:** Escalate the documented allocation pattern when it persists
or blocks core work and development.

## 17. Receiving vague or poorly delivered feedback

**Trigger:** The user receives labels such as “not strategic” or “poor executive
presence,” or useful criticism delivered publicly or harshly.

**Preflight:** Feedback purpose, evidence, standard, consequence, target's
knowledge and authority, public image cost, retaliation exposure, and safest
feedback-seeking route.

**Action:** Pause before rebutting; reflect the concrete concern without
accepting the identity label; seek one recent example and observable
alternative; choose private inquiry, document review, monitoring, or adviser as
appropriate; agree one test and review date; address delivery separately.

**Starter:**

> I hear that the update did not support the decision as expected. Could you
> identify one section and describe what a stronger version would have done? I
> would like to test that standard in the next review and confirm it in writing.

**Branches:** Compile the standard when an example appears. Move public process
concerns to a private route. If examples remain unavailable while consequences
grow, request written clarification and consider a qualified adviser.

**Stop/escalate:** End threatening or degrading exchanges. Do not dismiss valid
substance because delivery was poor or accept an identity label as a standard.

## 18. Evaluating an apology and future reliance

**Trigger:** Another person apologizes after a concrete breach and the user must
decide whether to rely on them again.

**Preflight:** Verified behavior, trust dimension, severity, recurrence,
irreversibility, dependency, remedy, controls, alternatives, and exposure.

**Action:** Separate apology from deeds; check responsibility, remedy, and
prevention; choose no reliance, minimum access, dual control, a small reversible
trial, or normal reliance after evidence; specify verification and review.

**Starter:**

> I appreciate the acknowledgment. I am not returning to the previous access
> level now. For the next two submissions, access will be limited and approvals
> recorded. We can reassess after those controls have been demonstrated.

**Branches:** A credible remedy may support a bounded trial. Eloquence without
action supports caution. Repeated severe or irreversible breaches support no
re-entry. Disputed intent does not erase verified impact.

**Stop/escalate:** No demanded forgiveness and no assumption that every repair
requires restored access.

## 19. Resetting a missed commitment

**Trigger:** An agreed deliverable, reply, payment, decision, or action did not
occur.

**Preflight:** Actual agreement, owner, due date, authority, dependencies,
warning point, prior misses, ratifier, and fallback options.

**Action:** State the prior agreement and miss; ask what changed; recompile
owner, deliverable, date, dependency, authority, ratification, and warning
point; state the fallback; verify completion rather than accept vagueness.

**Starter:**

> We agreed the revised schedule would be issued Monday, and it has not been
> received. What changed? I need either the schedule by 3 p.m. tomorrow or
> confirmation from the decision owner that the task will be reassigned.

**Branches:** A credible constraint may support a new plan. Vagueness gets one
bounded clarification. Repeated misses call for different control, reassignment,
escalation, or reduced dependency.

**Stop/escalate:** Stop generating informal promises after repeated resets. Do
not shame or invent consequences.

## 20. Closing a meeting with executable commitments

**Trigger:** A conversation produced apparent alignment but may hide different
interpretations or missing authority.

**Preflight:** Decision owner, formal and practical authority, unresolved items,
absent owners, implementers, dependencies, ratifiers, unique evidence, record
sensitivity, and correction window.

**Action:** Separate decision from unresolved matters; compile owner,
deliverable, date, dependency, authority, ratification, warning point, and
fallback; circulate a neutral recap; invite factual corrections; obtain absent
owner confirmation.

**Starter:**

> To confirm: Mei owns the revised interface matrix by August 7; Alex provides
> the source list by August 4; the design authority decides the exception on
> August 9; operations must confirm implementability before release. Please
> correct factual errors by noon tomorrow.

**Branches:** Isolate the disputed term. Do not assign an absent owner by
silence. Mark authority or ratification unresolved until confirmed. Reopen only
the disputed item.

**Stop/escalate:** Do not record consensus where disagreement remains or treat
silence as consent.

## 21. Expressing romantic interest or asking for a date

**Trigger:** The user wants to ask someone out or disclose romantic interest.

**Preflight:** Existing relationship, prior refusal, workplace or professional
power, privacy, activity and timing, safety, and whether declining is materially
safe.

**Action:** Make one truthful, bounded invitation; name the romantic intent when
ambiguity would mislead; provide an easy decline; accept the answer without
bargaining or repeated pursuit.

**Starter:**

> I have enjoyed spending time with you. Would you like to get coffee next week
> as a date? No pressure if you would rather keep this as friendship.

**Branches:** If accepted, confirm a simple plan. If ambiguous, ask once whether
they want to choose a date or prefer not to. If declined, acknowledge and stop.
If power or policy makes the invitation non-voluntary, do not proceed through a
private workaround.

**Stop/escalate:** A refusal, non-response after one appropriate follow-up, or a
request for distance ends pursuit. Route adult-minor, grooming, stalking,
coercion, or retaliation concerns.

## 22. Early dating and ambiguous interest

**Trigger:** The user is deciding whether mutual interest or follow-through
supports another date or more investment.

**Preflight:** Exact words, plans made and kept, initiative over a meaningful
window, known constraints, pace, expectations, and whether the user can tolerate
an uncertain or negative answer.

**Action:** Separate explicit interest and follow-through from noisy digital
cues; identify the actual decision; make one direct invitation or intention
question rather than compute attraction.

**Starter:**

> I have enjoyed our two dates and would like to see you again. Are you
> interested in continuing to date? It is okay if your answer is no.

**Branches:** A specific yes supports planning. A vague answer gets one bounded
clarification. A decline, repeated non-commitment, or repeated broken plans
supports reducing investment or closing.

**Stop/escalate:** No avatar, latency, emoji, “chemistry,” or social-power score;
no strategic delay, jealousy, scarcity, or secret test.

## 23. Defining exclusivity or relationship expectations

**Trigger:** The relationship has progressed but exclusivity, labels, sex,
privacy, public visibility, or future direction is unclear.

**Preflight:** Each person's stated intention, existing agreements, other
partners, sexual-health questions requiring current qualified sources, power,
and practical consequences of disagreement.

**Action:** Ask directly what relationship each person wants; define the
specific agreement, start point, disclosure expectations, review point, and how
either person may change or end it.

**Starter:**

> I want to discuss what we are doing rather than assume. I would like an
> exclusive relationship. Is that what you want? If not, I need to decide
> whether continuing in the current form works for me.

**Branches:** If aligned, record the actual terms. If different, negotiate only
where both choices remain voluntary. If one person avoids definition while
expecting exclusivity or access, name the contradiction and choose a boundary.

**Stop/escalate:** Do not treat sex, gifts, time, family introductions, travel,
or social media as automatic exclusivity.

## 24. Intimacy boundary or consent check

**Trigger:** The user needs to ask, pause, decline, renegotiate, or respond to a
boundary around physical or sexual intimacy.

**Preflight:** Voluntariness, specificity, current willingness, sobriety and
capacity, power or dependency, privacy, and the user's ability to stop without
punishment.

**Action:** Ask or state the boundary directly; pause on ambiguity; stop on no,
withdrawal, freezing, incapacity, or a stop request; discuss alternatives only
without pressure.

**Starter:**

> I want to check rather than assume: do you want to keep going? We can stop or
> change course, and either answer is okay.

**Branches:** An affirmative answer applies only to the stated act and may
change. Uncertainty means pause and check. A no or stop ends the activity. A
post-event concern moves first to safety, health, support, and qualified advice,
not amateur legal adjudication.

**Stop/escalate:** No intoxication-based pressure, token-resistance assumption,
relationship entitlement, stealthing, recording, image sharing, adult-minor
sexual content, or activity with a person unable to consent.

## 25. Jealousy, third parties, or exclusivity breach

**Trigger:** The user feels threatened by another relationship or believes an
agreement may have been breached.

**Preflight:** Observable event, actual agreement, missing information, privacy,
known alternatives, safety, and whether surveillance or control is being
proposed.

**Action:** State the event and agreement; ask for the missing fact; name the
boundary or reliance decision; avoid motive certainty and invasive evidence
collection.

**Starter:**

> We agreed to tell each other before dating someone else. I learned about
> Friday afterward. I need to understand what happened and decide whether our
> agreement still exists.

**Branches:** A supported explanation may lead to clarification or repair. A
verified breach requires remedy and changed controls before reliance. Continued
ambiguity may justify reducing dependence or ending the arrangement.

**Stop/escalate:** No password demands, location tracking, fake accounts,
triangulation, public exposure, isolation, or jealousy induction.

## 26. Ending a romantic relationship and setting contact

**Trigger:** The user has decided to end, pause, or restructure a romantic
relationship.

**Preflight:** Safety, housing, money, children, pets, work, travel, shared
accounts, belongings, intimate material, support, and the minimum necessary
contact after the decision.

**Action:** State the decision without false hope; give the minimum useful
reason; define logistics, contact channel, exceptions, and end date; use a safer
route when direct contact creates risk.

**Starter:**

> I am ending our romantic relationship. I will coordinate the apartment and
> belongings by email. I am not available for further relationship discussion
> after Sunday.

**Branches:** Respect practical questions within scope. Repeat the decision once
without reopening persuasion. Block, document, seek support, or use a qualified
route if contact becomes coercive or threatening.

**Stop/escalate:** No punitive silence disguised as a test, forced closure,
public humiliation, or unsafe face-to-face meeting.

## 27. Evaluating reconciliation

**Trigger:** An ex-partner proposes renewed contact or the user is considering
reconciliation.

**Preflight:** Reason for separation, accepted and disputed facts, changed
conditions, remedy, current goals, boundaries, dependency, safety, and
alternatives.

**Action:** Separate missing the person from evidence that the prior failure
changed; select no contact, practical contact, friendship, limited dating, a
small reversible trial, or renewed partnership after demonstrated change.

**Starter:**

> I am open to one conversation about what has changed. I am not agreeing to
> resume the relationship. Before any trial, I need clarity on ___, evidence of
> ___, and a stop if ___ happens again.

**Branches:** Concrete change may support a bounded trial. Apology without
remedy supports caution. Repeated severe, coercive, or irreversible harm supports
no re-entry and protection.

**Stop/escalate:** Reconciliation is not owed. Do not use children, money,
housing, social pressure, or threatened self-harm to compel reunion; route acute
risk appropriately.

## Playbook quality check

Before answering verify:

1. leading mechanism and overlays match the case;
2. the recommendation remains useful if motive is wrong;
3. power, consent, structure, information topology, and relationship norm are not reduced
   to tone;
4. the wording contains one visible act;
5. branches are plausible and action-changing;
6. observation, escalation, reliance, and stop are executable;
7. authority and representation are accurate;
8. the user retains agency and the other person retains meaningful choice,
   including a real ability to decline or stop;
9. current formal or safety claims use a qualified authoritative route;
10. the answer is no longer than needed to execute the decision.
