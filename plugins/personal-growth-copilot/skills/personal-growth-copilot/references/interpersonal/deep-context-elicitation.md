# Interpersonal context and bounded models

Use [shared inquiry](../collaborative-inquiry.md) for readiness, ask/wait, depth consent, quick mode, correction and stopping. Do not run a second interview.

## Choose the material gap


These are choices, not a checklist to administer to everyone.

| Gap | Useful question | Decision it can change |
|---|---|---|
| Objective | What would a workable outcome look like after your next interaction? | Repair, clarification, boundary, or exit |
| Label replacing evidence | What happened in the most recent instance, in their words if you recall? | Whether the concern is wording, conduct, or interpretation |
| Power/dependency | What can this person actually decide about your work or access? | Directness, audience, protection |
| Pattern | What happened when you raised this before? | First clarification versus repeated-boundary response |
| User contribution | What did you tell them you needed, and what was their response? | Unspoken expectation versus ignored agreement |
| Conflicting goals | If protecting the relationship and getting this outcome conflict, which matters more here? | The real trade-off |
| Contradiction | Earlier you said they had agreed; now it sounds like they only acknowledged receipt. What did they actually commit to? | Request versus accountability |
| Constraint | What consequence could you realistically follow through on? | Enforceable boundary versus empty threat |

Challenge one consequential assumption at a time. Link the challenge to evidence
and allow correction. Do not cross-examine suffering, blame someone for another
person's abuse, or insist on an alternative to an explicit refusal. Support can
mean validating an emotion while questioning the explanation or proposed move.


## Bounded accounts of people


When useful, infer bounded interaction tendencies and relationship dynamics,
not hidden essence.

Prefer:

> Across the three deadline incidents you described, Jordan took decisions into
> a smaller forum after disagreement. A plausible working hypothesis is that
> under time pressure Jordan centralizes control. Confidence: moderate. This
> matters because a written decision-right check may work better than debating
> intent. It would weaken if comparable urgent decisions remained open and
> consultative.

Avoid:

> Jordan is controlling and will always undermine you.

For every material actor or relationship hypothesis record:

| Field | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Observed basis | Repeated words, actions, follow-through, records, or local comparison |
| Working hypothesis | Context-bounded tendency or relationship dynamic |
| Confidence | Low, moderate, or high; never pseudo-precise |
| Decision relevance | What choice changes if the hypothesis is useful |
| Alternatives | Strongest evidence-linked competing explanation |
| Falsifier | Observable event that weakens or replaces the hypothesis |

Require more than one ambiguous cue before describing a pattern. Do not infer
personality from gaze, posture, voice, response delay, nationality, language,
disability, neurotype, age, gender, or other group averages. Do not diagnose,
assign MBTI or attachment labels, decide honesty or consent, score
manipulability or influence, or let every response confirm the theory. Record a
typology only when the user supplies it as optional reflective vocabulary.

Relationship modeling may describe observable dynamics such as:

- who initiates, follows through, repairs, avoids, decides, or carries cost;
- whether expectations are explicit, hidden, reciprocal, communal, disputed,
  or changing;
- whether trust, access, reliance, conflict, or audience exposure is increasing
  or decreasing;
- whether words and behavior converge over time.

Use these as revisable decision aids, not moral verdicts.


For retained case content use [profiles and scoring](profiles-and-scoring.md), governed by [shared continuity](../memory-and-continuity.md). Session-only is the default. A supplied account never establishes a private motive or fixed personality.
