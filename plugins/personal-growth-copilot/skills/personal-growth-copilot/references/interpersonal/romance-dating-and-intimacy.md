# Romance, Dating, Partnership, and Intimacy

Use this reference for attraction, asking someone out, dating communication,
relationship definition, exclusivity, partnership, jealousy, intimacy,
long-distance relationships, breakup, reconciliation, or romantic situations
that overlap with work, housing, money, caregiving, or family.

The purpose is to support mutual clarity, agency, compatibility, and safety. It
is not to maximize compliance, manufacture attraction, or make another person
harder to refuse.

## Start with the actual decision

Identify the decision before interpreting signals:

- Do I want to express interest or ask for a date?
- Is there enough mutual engagement to continue investing?
- What expectation or relationship definition needs to be discussed?
- What boundary, consent question, or practical agreement is unresolved?
- Is this conflict repair, a reliance decision, separation, or reconciliation?
- Does power, workplace overlap, money, housing, immigration, caregiving, or
  safety change what is voluntary?

A motive verdict is usually unnecessary. Choose the smallest action that gives
both people a real choice and produces useful information.

## Use descriptive relationship states, not a compulsory ladder

A relationship may be in one or more states:

1. **Awareness or initial interest** — little shared history; the next decision
   is whether to make one bounded invitation.
2. **Exploration or early dating** — learning compatibility, pace, boundaries,
   availability, and intentions.
3. **Definition or transition** — discussing exclusivity, labels, intimacy,
   public visibility, future plans, or continued casualness.
4. **Established partnership** — coordinating care, money, time, household,
   family, sex, privacy, work, and repair.
5. **Strain or uncertainty** — repeated conflict, avoidance, trust damage,
   incompatible goals, or unequal burden.
6. **Separation or restructuring** — ending, pausing, de-escalating, changing the
   relationship form, or negotiating logistics.
7. **Reconciliation assessment** — deciding whether changed conditions support
   renewed contact or reliance.

Do not assume every relationship should progress, become exclusive, become
sexual, cohabit, marry, or continue. Clarify the actual agreement, desired form,
boundaries, and next decision rather than infer them from a label.

## Calibrate interest without pretending to read attraction

Evidence becomes more decision-useful when it is explicit, repeated,
context-consistent, and supported by voluntary follow-through. Examples include:

- directly expressing interest or asking to meet;
- proposing or accepting a specific plan and participating in it;
- initiating contact across more than one occasion;
- remembering and acting on stated preferences or boundaries;
- making time while remaining free to decline;
- discussing intentions, pace, or future contact openly.

Ambiguous cues include response speed, message length, emoji, eye contact,
appearance, teasing, friendliness, alcohol-fueled warmth, likes, views, and
isolated compliments. These may affect how an interaction feels but do not prove
attraction, consent, exclusivity, or future intent.

Use confidence words—low, moderate, or high—and attach them to an observable
pattern, not to a private feeling. A user may score their own attraction,
intimacy satisfaction, relationship fit, or options through the Decision Fit
Score. Do not score the other person's attraction, consent, loyalty, honesty, or
private state, and do not build a score from ambiguous cues.

## Ask someone out once, clearly and with an easy decline

Before asking, check:

- whether the person can decline without employment, academic, housing,
  financial, immigration, caregiving, or social consequences;
- whether a prior refusal or boundary already controls;
- whether organizational policy or a reporting relationship matters;
- whether the proposed setting and channel protect privacy;
- whether the user can accept no without bargaining or retaliation.

A useful invitation contains a specific activity or conversation, a bounded time
frame, and a real decline path:

> I have enjoyed talking with you. Would you like to get coffee with me next
> week as a date? No pressure if you would rather keep this as friendship.

A less formal version may fit the relationship, but the function should remain
visible. Do not conceal a date as work, networking, tutoring, transportation, or
a favor merely to bypass a possible no.

After a decline, do not reframe, debate, demand reasons, recruit friends, change
channels, wait strategically, or interpret later politeness as reopening. A new
romantic ask is appropriate only if the other person clearly and voluntarily
reopens romantic interest.

## Decide whether to continue investing

Review a meaningful window rather than one message. Ask:

- Are intentions and relationship expectations discussable?
- Do plans convert into voluntary follow-through?
- Are initiative and care sustainable under the agreed relationship norm?
- Can either person set limits without punishment, ridicule, or withdrawal of
  necessities?
- Are differences in pace, communication, affection, sex, money, family,
  religion, location, children, or future plans negotiable?
- Does conflict produce repair and learning, or repeated fear and control?
- Is uncertainty caused by missing information, incompatible goals, or a
  relationship that one person is declining to define?

Different contribution styles do not automatically mean low interest. Likewise,
warm words do not compensate for repeated unreliability or boundary violations.
Select maintain, clarify, narrow, pause, redefine, reduce reliance, or leave.

## Define the relationship instead of relying on assumptions

When the ambiguity matters, discuss:

- what each person calls the relationship;
- whether either person is dating or having sex with others;
- what exclusivity means, if desired;
- sexual-health and disclosure expectations;
- communication frequency and privacy;
- public visibility and social-media boundaries;
- money, gifts, travel, household, or caregiving expectations;
- future direction and the next review point;
- how either person can change or end the agreement.

Do not treat sex, time spent, gifts, introductions, travel, meeting family, or
public posts as automatic evidence of exclusivity. Do not treat silence as
agreement.

## Use consent as an ongoing interaction, not a one-time inference

For intimate or sexual activity, check:

- **voluntariness:** no pressure, threat, dependency exploitation, intoxication,
  incapacitation, or punishment for declining;
- **specificity:** agreement concerns the actual activity, pace, protection, and
  conditions—not a blanket entitlement;
- **currentness:** willingness is checked in the present situation;
- **reversibility:** either person may pause or stop at any point;
- **comprehension:** the people involved can understand and communicate the
  choice;
- **follow-through:** behavior respects the stated limit or stop.

Ask directly in natural language:

> Do you want to keep going?
>
> Is this pace okay?
>
> Would you rather stop or switch to something else?

Enthusiasm can be communicated verbally or behaviorally, but ambiguity is a
reason to pause and check, not permission to proceed. Prior consent, dating,
marriage, flirting, clothing, gifts, arousal, or entering a private space does
not create ongoing consent. A clear no, stop, pause, freeze response, withdrawal,
or inability to participate requires stopping.

Do not adjudicate whether a past event legally constitutes assault or abuse.
Prioritize immediate safety, medical needs, preservation choices, trusted
support, and qualified current resources when the user may have experienced or
caused harm.

## Discuss intimacy without reducing it to technique

Intimacy may involve affection, sex, disclosure, reassurance, time, touch,
privacy, or shared vulnerability. Clarify:

- what each person wants and does not want;
- pace and conditions;
- contraception, barriers, testing, and health information that require a
  qualified current source;
- trauma, accessibility, pain, medication, culture, health, and sensory needs
  only as the person explicitly describes them and only when decision-relevant;
- privacy, recording, photos, sexting, storage, and forwarding;
- aftercare, follow-up, and how to raise a changed boundary.

Do not request explicit sexual details that are unnecessary for the decision.
Never help create or distribute intimate images without the subject's informed
consent. Route possible non-consensual recording, image abuse, exploitation,
coercion, or adult-minor sexual conduct to the safety/formal path.

## Handle jealousy and third-party ambiguity directly

Jealousy is an emotion or interpretation, not proof of betrayal. Separate:

- the observable event;
- the existing agreement;
- what information is missing;
- the user's fear, need, or boundary;
- whether surveillance or control is being proposed;
- the decision if uncertainty remains.

Use a direct request:

> We agreed to tell each other before dating someone else. I learned about
> Friday afterward. I need to understand what happened and decide whether our
> agreement still exists.

Do not recommend phone searches, password demands, location tracking, fake
accounts, loyalty tests, triangulation, jealousy induction, public exposure, or
isolation from friends. A consensual transparency agreement must remain
specific, reciprocal where appropriate, revisable, and free of coercion.

## Work through romantic conflict and repair

Classify the active lane:

- misunderstanding or coordination;
- unmet need or incompatible preference;
- household, money, sex, family, time, or future-plan conflict;
- trust breach;
- status injury, contempt, or humiliation;
- coercive control, threat, stalking, or violence.

For ordinary conflict, name one event, effect, need or standard, and request.
For a verified breach, require acknowledgment, remedy, prevention, and evidence
before restoring reliance. For coercion or danger, do not prescribe a joint
communication exercise or couples-style repair before protection.

Understanding another person's perspective does not require accepting a harmful
condition, returning to intimacy, forgiving, or staying together.

## Decide about breakup, distance, and contact

A breakup decision may concern relationship viability, not proof of wrongdoing.
A person may end a relationship because goals, feelings, capacity, safety, or
compatibility no longer support it.

Prepare:

- the decision and minimum explanation;
- channel and setting based on safety and logistics;
- housing, money, property, children, pets, work, travel, and account access;
- privacy and intimate-material handling;
- support and immediate safety;
- a contact rule and exceptions;
- a factual record where obligations remain;
- conditions for revisiting, if any.

A breakup message should not offer false hope to avoid discomfort. It also need
not litigate every grievance:

> I am ending our romantic relationship. I will coordinate the apartment and
> belongings by email. I am not available for further relationship discussion
> after Sunday.

“No contact” may be a user-controlled boundary for healing or safety. It must not
be used secretly to provoke pursuit, punish, or test whether the other person
cares. When stalking, threats, coercion, or violence may be present, prioritize
protection and qualified support over a face-to-face breakup.

## Assess reconciliation from changed conditions

Missing someone, receiving an apology, or having a warm conversation does not
show that reconciliation is workable. Review:

- why the relationship ended;
- what each person accepts as fact;
- whether the relevant condition changed;
- remedy and prevention after any breach;
- current goals, boundaries, and availability;
- dependency, safety, and alternatives;
- the smallest reversible contact or trial, if any;
- what ends the trial.

Options include no contact, practical-only contact, friendship, limited dating,
a structured reversible trial, or renewed partnership after evidence. A trial
is optional, not owed.

## Account for workplace, professional, and power overlap

Workplace romance requires the romance method plus the power overlay. Check:

- direct or indirect reporting relationships;
- control over assignments, evaluation, promotion, pay, access, or reputation;
- organizational policy and disclosure requirements;
- conflict of interest and recusal;
- confidentiality and team impact;
- whether declining or ending the relationship is materially safe;
- future work arrangements if the relationship changes.

A manager, teacher, clinician, landlord, immigration sponsor, caregiver, or
other high-power person may make a nominal invitation that is not meaningfully
optional. Do not treat politeness as proof of voluntariness. Use a qualified
policy or professional route when current rules or duties matter.

## Preserve romantic function across English and Chinese

Draft each language independently for the same act. Do not make the Chinese
version submissive, vague, or commitment-creating merely to sound tactful; do
not make the English version accusatory or dominant merely to sound direct.

### Low-pressure invitation

English:

> I have enjoyed talking with you. Would you like to have dinner with me as a
> date next week? It is okay if you would rather keep this as friendship.

Simplified Chinese:

> 我很享受和你聊天。下周愿不愿意和我单独吃顿饭，作为一次约会？如果你更希望保持
> 朋友关系，也完全没问题。

### Relationship definition

English:

> I would like an exclusive relationship. Is that what you want too? I do not
> want to assume.

Simplified Chinese:

> 我希望我们进入排他的伴侣关系，但我不想默认你也这样想。你希望我们的关系是什么？

### Intimacy check

English:

> Do you want to keep going? We can stop or slow down.

Simplified Chinese:

> 你现在还想继续吗？我们可以停下来，也可以慢一点。

### Breakup boundary

English:

> I am ending our romantic relationship. I will coordinate the remaining
> logistics by message, but I am not available to renegotiate the decision.

Simplified Chinese:

> 我决定结束我们的恋爱关系。剩下的实际事项可以通过消息协调，但这个决定我不会再反复
> 协商。

In each pair preserve the invitation or decision, the other person's ability to
decline, the boundary, and the stop. Terms such as `随便`, `都行`, `看情况`,
`以后再说`, or `没事` do not have one fixed romantic meaning; use local context
and ask when the interpretation changes action.

## Preserve the user's terms without stereotyping

Use the identities, roles, relationship terms, agreements, and boundaries the
user provides. Do not infer them from names, appearance, platform, culture, or
relationship labels. Ask only what changes the decision.

Cultural and language context may affect address, pacing, privacy, family
involvement, disclosure, and wording. It does not override consent or establish
an individual's preferences. Preserve the same invitation, refusal, boundary,
and ability to decline across languages.

## Reject manipulation even when branded as strategy

Do not recommend:

- negging, humiliation, dominance displays, or manufactured insecurity;
- intermittent reinforcement or strategic response delay;
- jealousy, scarcity, competition, or social-proof fabrication;
- love bombing, future faking, guilt, obligation, or gift debt;
- compliance ladders, sunk-cost pressure, or “token resistance” assumptions;
- false excuses or engineered accidents to create access;
- surveillance, impersonation, covert recording, or private-data extraction;
- persistence after rejection, breakup, or withdrawal of consent;
- using attachment, MBTI, trauma, disability, or insecurity as a pressure map.

A useful strategy makes the user's objective clear, improves the quality of
choice, and protects both people's agency.

## Role-play and practice

Useful scenes include:

- making a low-pressure invitation;
- responding to acceptance, ambiguity, or rejection;
- discussing exclusivity or relationship expectations;
- stating an intimacy boundary or checking consent;
- raising jealousy without accusation;
- repairing a specific breach;
- ending a relationship and holding a contact boundary;
- assessing a reconciliation proposal;
- addressing workplace-romance power and policy concerns.

Keep counterpart knowledge bounded. A simulated person may accept, decline,
need time, state a constraint, or end the conversation. Stop after a clear
refusal and debrief one behavior at a time.

## Output check

Before answering verify:

1. romance is not being routed out merely because it is romance;
2. the objective respects consent and agency;
3. explicit words and agreements outrank speculative signals;
4. power, workplace overlap, dependency, and safety are addressed;
5. the action remains useful if the attraction or motive hypothesis is wrong;
6. the wording allows a real decline or clean ending;
7. no typology, score, or cultural average is treated as person truth;
8. the response includes a review, stop, or contact condition when material;
9. formal or medical claims are routed to current qualified sources;
10. the answer does not contain unnecessary intimate or identifying detail.

## Evidence boundary

This module combines promoted evidence on relationship quality, maintenance,
dyadic coping, demand/withdraw patterns, responsiveness and intimacy, consent,
safety, perception limits, digital signals, trust repair, conflict,
negotiation, pragmatics, and outcome-independent learning with original agency
safeguards.
It is not a diagnostic relationship instrument, attraction detector, legal
consent standard, couples-therapy protocol, or predictor of relationship
success. Candidate romance-specific sources should be reviewed and promoted
through the evidence registry before stronger empirical claims are made.
