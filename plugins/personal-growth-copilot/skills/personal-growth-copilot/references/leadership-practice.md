# Leadership practice

Use within an explicitly invoked Personal Growth Copilot session. These are portable
adaptations of Leadership Toolkit 1.1.0, including its October 12, 2026 working changes.
[Programme and provenance](leadership-programme.md) explains source status and limits.

## Start with the user's job

Offer the requested kind of help: explain a method, understand an episode, choose a
response, prepare a conversation, rehearse, or review an attempt. For a lookup, answer
directly. For a live problem, ask only the missing question most likely to change the
next action. Do not require the whole table or a personal history.

| Situation | Distinction that changes the response | Starting route |
|---|---|---|
| Work keeps coming back to me | Missing rights, knowledge, capacity, or options? | L01 for rights/support; demonstrate or pair for knowledge; L09 for capacity; L02 for thinking through options |
| I want to grow beyond technical execution | What contribution is needed, and which previous duties can move? | Role transitions in programme notes; L01 and team capability below |
| Several worthwhile directions compete | Is the outcome still open, or are fixed commitments overloaded? | L12 for direction; L09 for workload; L04 for incompatible objectives |
| A difficult conversation | Observed behavior, contested account, or conflicting interests? | L03 feedback; L11 evidence; L04 negotiation |
| Quieter people are not contributing | Can they disagree freely, and can input alter the decision? | L05 if conditions permit; another input route or protected process if not |
| Instructions or priorities changed | Missing facts, contradictory authority, or uncertain effect? | Retrieve facts; seek owner reconciliation/L04; L08 for an authorized test; L06 to reconnect the plan |
| I need cooperation beyond my authority | What can the other party actually decide, and what remains open? | L10 evidence and consultation |
| I feel pressured or stuck | Immediate attention, or unsustainable conditions? | Pressure practice below; L09 capacity; L07 for a recurring coordination problem |
| An attempt did not work | What was attempted, observed, and experienced by others? | L07 and the short practice note below |

Card IDs refer to [leadership-methods.md](leadership-methods.md). Keep alternatives
visible; the library does not require choosing a card or intervening.

## Match before acting

Separate the job a method could help with from whether it can be used here. For
material constraints, check who can decide, task-specific knowledge, realistic
capacity, affected people's accounts, freedom to participate, and required protections.
An unknown prerequisite calls for clarification, not a favorable assumption.

Recommend a method with a reason and the strongest plausible alternative. State
whether it is usable now, conditional on a named prerequisite, unsuitable, unresolved,
or unnecessary. Conditional means wait for the prerequisite before acting. Formal
leadership does not establish competence or capacity; informal leadership does not
grant authority. A proposed handoff is not an agreement.

Do not rate people's potential or use a numerical method score as a prediction of
success. Keep unknown, contested, and not applicable distinct. Consult the canonical
MATCHING.md only if the user needs its fuller comparison procedure; ordinary use
requires no score or access to the source repository.

## Explain, rehearse, and transfer

For learning, explain one distinction with a short fictional example, then ask the
user to choose or draft a next action before showing a complete answer. Respond to
what they actually wrote: identify one effective choice, one consequential omission,
and a possible revision. Offer a second situation with a changed constraint so they
can explain when they would switch methods. Familiarity with card names is not the aim.

For rehearsal, agree who the copilot will play and the useful challenge. Play one
exchange at a time; let the user respond. Mark the counterpart's reactions as simulated,
not predictions or evidence of that person's motives. Pause when requested. Debrief
clarity, authority, listening, and feasibility; let the user try a revision. Do not
reward winning agreement through pressure or pretending a fixed decision is open.

Close with one next action or unresolved question and what would show whether it
helped. Offer a later review when useful; do not create reminders, contact colleagues,
assign work, or save records merely because a method mentions follow-up.

## Team capability and continuity

Start with one delivery responsibility rather than a person-rating exercise:

1. Describe the outcome, required checks, and dated evidence of current task capability,
   including conditions and support. Mark missing evidence as unknown.
2. Identify current and backup coverage. A named backup or completed course does not
   establish that someone can perform the work.
3. Ask the prospective learner about interest, task experience, capacity, and support.
   Choose demonstration, paired practice, observation, or independent work to fit this
   task and its consequences.
4. Agree a bounded opportunity, decision rights, available help, and protected time for
   both learner and supporter. Identify displaced work; use L09 before adding overload.
5. At an agreed real checkpoint, look for a decision they can explain, useful evidence,
   required quality, appropriate escalation, and hidden rework or support effort.

Keep retained accountability and independent checking explicit. Record agreed,
declined, or unresolved ownership. This is a local learning worksheet, not a personnel
appraisal, succession ranking, or official competency framework. It applies the source
role-and-team review and Leading people overview, not a validated capability measure.

## Pressure and sustainable contribution

Work with the user's own reported experience: notice an early tell, pause if the task
allows, clarify the actual decision, widen the picture to missing information or help,
and choose one familiar cue or useful next action. Afterwards ask whether the approach
helped and whether workload, recovery, coordination, or support needs changing.

Do not infer another person's state from silence or behavior. Do not turn structural
overload into a resilience or motivation deficit. The programme's pressure curves,
physiology explanations, and wellbeing suggestions are not personal measurements or
clinical protocols; use the existing safety/scope route when needed.

## Short practice note

Offer only if a take-away or review is useful. This is a conversational preview;
[session-capsule.md](session-capsule.md) and the existing host consent contract govern
any save. Minimize colleague identifiers. Never automatically update the source library.

- **Situation and aim:** date, whose account, desired change, existing baseline if any.
- **Planned response:** card/source version, reason, prediction, authority, prerequisites,
  support, and stop condition. Label an expectation reconstructed afterwards.
- **Actual action:** attempted, changed, declined, deferred, or abandoned; what happened.
- **Observation:** dated evidence; separate a completed output from a later result or unknown.
- **Other effects:** other accounts or their absence, quality, hidden effort, unintended effects.
- **Next judgment:** keep, adapt, switch, or stop and why; any already agreed checkpoint.

For a deliberate test, use the existing behavior-change experiment reference to make
the prediction, comparator, observation window, and stop condition explicit before
acting. One favorable episode does not validate the method or matching framework.
