# Leadership practice guides

Portable synthesis of the seven existing source-library guides, inspected 23
October 2026. These are editorial adaptations, not validated workplace protocols.
Use one immediate job and one relevant method. [Source/edition limits](leadership-programme.md)
apply; the source notebook and original books are not required runtime files.

## Self and direction

Clarify what contribution is needed now, the user's values and fixed commitments,
and which decisions or responsibilities are actually open. Keep technical depth,
client responsiveness and enabling others in view rather than treating management
as a ladder of personal worth. Compare stop/keep/start choices against authority,
capacity and effects on others. Use [strategic choice](leadership/cards/L12-strategic-choice.md)
when direction is open and [capacity](leadership/cards/L09-capacity.md) when it is fixed.
Reported course attendance, aspiration or a role title is not outcome evidence.

## Develop others and team capability

Name the delivery outcome, evidence of task-specific capability and current/backup
coverage. Ask the prospective learner about interest, rights, knowledge, time and
support; naming a backup does not prove coverage. Distinguish instruction/pairing,
[delegation](leadership/cards/L01-delegation.md) and [coaching](leadership/cards/L02-grow.md).
Protect practice and support time; identify what work moves to make room. Set a
bounded opportunity with retained accountability and required independent checks.
Look for explainable judgments, quality, available support and hidden rework at a
real checkpoint; fewer questions can mean withdrawal. Return the next action to
its owner after supplying help instead of silently taking the work back.

For a one-to-one, agree the useful job, hear the person's account, explore the
task/support needs, choose a next action and check the relationship between
manager support and actual autonomy. This adapts Grove and Bungay Stanier; a
coaching script cannot supply missing knowledge, authority or capacity.

## Communication and trust

When a plan changes, state the previous and new purpose, decision authority,
what stops/continues and the consequences for dependencies. A sent message is
not shared understanding. Use [brief/huddle](leadership/cards/L06-brief-huddle.md)
and ask people to explain the next action and remaining conflict. Use
[feedback](leadership/cards/L03-feedback.md) to discuss observable behaviour
and its impact, hear intent/context and retain disputes without motive labels.
Clarify appreciation, coaching or evaluation when receiving feedback. Acknowledge
reports without endorsing every claim, explain what input changed, and preserve
an alternative channel or protected route when participation is unsafe. The
ability to speak freely cannot be created by a facilitation format alone.

## Change and influence

Separate missing facts (retrieve/ask), uncertain effect (a bounded authorized
test), and disputed objectives/authority (a decision to reconcile). Use
[influence](leadership/cards/L10-influence.md), [negotiation](leadership/cards/L04-negotiation.md)
or [PDSA](leadership/cards/L08-pdsa.md) accordingly. State fixed parts of a decision
honestly; consultation must concern something actually open. Check concurrent
changes, lived culture, pace/history and workload before assigning unwillingness.

Distinguish technical work that expertise can resolve from adaptive work requiring
people to change practices or priorities. Mixed cases are normal. Ask affected
people what they must preserve and could lose; do not invent hidden loyalties or
manufacture conflict. These are Heifetz-informed questions, not authority to act.

For a self-chosen change that stalls, use Kegan/Lahey's four-column map:
improvement goal → contrary behaviour → possible competing commitment → big
assumption. The user supplies their own account; never assign hidden commitments
to a colleague. Select a small assumption test with prediction, observation and
acceptable downside. Their SMART means safe/modest, actionable, and a research
stance testing an assumption; distinguish it from goal-setting SMART. A map
makes a hypothesis inspectable, not proven false or clinically established.

## Pressure and sustainable contribution

Use the user's reported early signals to pause where the task permits, clarify
the actual decision, widen attention to facts/help and choose one familiar next
action. Examine workload, recovery, coordination and support afterwards. Do not
translate overload into a resilience deficit or infer another person's state
from silence. Programme pressure curves and physiology descriptions are not
personal measurements or clinical advice. Use [capacity](leadership/cards/L09-capacity.md)
for demand/resource conflicts and existing safety/professional routes when needed.

## Reflection and lasting contribution

Separate intent, action, outcome, other accounts, cost and learning. Use the
[practice note](leadership/practice-note.md), [debrief](leadership/cards/L07-debrief.md)
and [shared review](reflection-and-review.md). Keep, adapt, switch or stop with a
reason; absence of follow-up is unknown. A retrospective expectation is labelled
as reconstructed. A leadership story can describe experience, changed thinking
and intended action without inventing changed workplace outcomes. No recurring
schedule or new assignment follows automatically from reflection.

## Technical leadership and architecture contribution

Preserve the decision state: a completed model establishes artifact completion,
not technical readiness, a preferred option or an approved design. With missing
criteria/evidence, propose an evidence-gathering or validation decision and retain
a pending design decision. Label any later decision-record wording as a conditional
template until an authorized decision is observed.


Start with the customer/operational outcome and consequential decision, not a
preferred model or tool. Locate the obstacle in the technical concept, working
arrangement, stakeholder understanding or authority. Weinberg's motivation,
organization and ideas lens supplies questions about the work, not a diagnosis.
Muller's business-to-realization viewpoints help reconnect purpose and technical
choices; Maier/Rechtin supports examining consequential choices and independent
participants. Preserve assumptions, alternative options, dissent and required
assurance. A complete model is not an accepted decision.

Prepare a decision brief: problem/outcome, options, technical/business effects,
evidence and uncertainty, recommendation and explicit decision sought. Obtain
audience-aware challenge without hiding risk or assuming lack of expertise.
Use [L10](leadership/cards/L10-influence.md) and [L12](leadership/cards/L12-strategic-choice.md).

Larson's Tech Lead, Architect, Solver and Right Hand patterns are prompts to
clarify work, scope, manager partnership, support, escalation and thinking time,
not a universal career ladder. Pair mentoring with sponsorship: suitable work,
access and visibility while retaining the learner's ownership. For quality,
define the property and observable evidence, find the concentrated problem and
try a proportionate improvement before adding process. Review coordination cost
and proxy limitations. A proposed change does not replace formal requirements.

The source library also includes NASA and Staff Engineer reader captures; they
inform discussion prompts, not personal trait scores or certification. Original
book editions and source-record limitations remain in the provenance guide.
