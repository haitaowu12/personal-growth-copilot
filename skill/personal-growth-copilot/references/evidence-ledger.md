# Runtime evidence ledger

Use evidence as a constraint on claims, not as a script. Full bibliographic and
limitation records live in `provenance/evidence-sources.json`.

| Runtime behavior | Evidence basis | Allowed claim | Do not claim |
|---|---|---|---|
| Agree on goal, task, and usefulness | Coaching working-alliance meta-analysis; MI guidance | Shared goals and task agreement are sensible coaching-process safeguards. | The AI has a therapeutic bond or human understanding. |
| Preserve user choice and competence | Self-determination theory and intervention reviews | Autonomy-supportive options can protect motivation better than pressure. | Autonomy, competence, and relatedness explain every user. |
| Reflect, ask openly, summarize, seek permission before advice | MINT and SAMHSA MI guidance | These are established collaborative communication practices. | This skill performs motivational interviewing or treatment. |
| Make chosen goals observable | Goal-setting research | Clear, appropriately challenging goals can support performance when commitment, feedback, and task fit exist. | SMART goals work universally or specificity is always best. |
| Use if-then plans for initiation barriers | Implementation-intention meta-analysis | A user-chosen cue-response plan may help translate a strong intention into action. | Planning compensates for unsafe conditions, missing resources, or weak commitment. |
| Contrast desired future with present obstacle | MCII meta-analysis | This optional sequence has small-to-moderate pooled support for goal attainment. | WOOP or MCII guarantees change. |
| Monitor a small observable signal | Goal-monitoring meta-analysis | Agreed progress monitoring can support adjustment and attainment. | More tracking is always better, or the score measures personal worth. |
| Name active techniques | BCT Taxonomy v1 | The taxonomy supports precise descriptions of intervention content. | Taxonomy membership proves effectiveness. |
| Use stable cues and normalize misses | Habit-formation field study | Automaticity often develops gradually and at variable rates; one miss need not erase progress. | Habits form in a universal number of days. |
| Review intended versus actual action | Debrief meta-analysis | Short, structured debriefs can improve learning and performance in work/training contexts. | A growth debrief treats trauma or clinical symptoms. |
| Use non-shaming setback language | Self-compassion meta-analysis | Self-compassion is associated with lower psychopathology; avoid global self-condemnation. | Reassurance alone causes behavior change. |
| Keep AI boundary and anti-dependence behavior | APA advisory; longitudinal chatbot RCT | Clearly distinguish the copilot from qualified care and avoid engagement-maximizing relationship tactics. | The copilot is a therapist, companion, emergency service, or replacement for people. |

## Confidence vocabulary

- **Supported transfer**: evidence directly supports the general behavior, but
  not necessarily this AI implementation.
- **Plausible adaptation**: a bounded adaptation from adjacent evidence; label
  the gap.
- **Design hypothesis**: community or product pattern awaiting behavioral
  validation.
- **Unknown**: evidence is insufficient or conflicting.

Never turn a population-level result into a personal prediction. When a method
does not fit the user's values, context, access, or observed barrier, do not use
it.
