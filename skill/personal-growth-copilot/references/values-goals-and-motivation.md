# Values, goals, and motivation

## Values clarification

Treat values as user-authored directions, not a fixed inventory or moral rank.
Ask about lived choices and tradeoffs rather than abstract labels alone:

- “What would this choice protect or make possible?”
- “If no one rewarded or punished either option, which would you endorse?”
- “What cost are you willing—and unwilling—to pay?”
- “Whose standard is operating here?”
- “What would enough look like?”

Separate:

- value from social expectation;
- direction from measurable goal;
- chosen constraint from imposed constraint;
- desire from obligation;
- identity aspiration from current behavior.

Use autonomy, competence, and relatedness as optional prompts. Do not claim they
exhaust motivation or that a low area diagnoses a problem.

## Goal quality

Before making a goal specific, determine its type:

- **exploration:** reduce uncertainty through prototypes or information;
- **learning:** acquire or practice a capability;
- **performance:** achieve an observable result;
- **maintenance:** preserve a useful state or routine;
- **recovery:** reduce load and restore capacity;
- **decision:** choose or intentionally defer by a defined point.

Specific performance targets can impair early exploration or complex learning.
Choose the right goal type before choosing the metric.

Check:

1. ownership: does the user endorse it?
2. meaning: why does it matter now?
3. clarity: what observable change would count?
4. feasibility: what resources, skills, and opportunity exist?
5. difficulty: challenging enough to matter, not detached from reality;
6. feedback: what evidence will arrive soon enough to adjust?
7. conflict: which other commitments compete?
8. exit: when should the goal be revised, paused, or abandoned?

## Ambivalence

Do not defeat ambivalence. Map it.

```text
Reasons to change
Reasons not to change
Costs of change
Costs of no change
What each side protects
What evidence would move the decision
```

Reflect both sides fairly. Ask for the user's own reasons before adding yours.
Advice is allowed when requested; distinguish information from persuasion.

## Importance and confidence scales

If useful, ask separately:

- importance of this goal, from 0 “not worth pursuing now” to 10 “one of my
  highest current priorities”;
- confidence in this action, from 0 “I do not expect to attempt it” to 10 “I
  expect to do it even in a difficult week.”

Then ask why the number is not lower and what would raise it one point. The
number is a conversation aid, not a psychological measurement.

## Choice

Offer two or three viable paths with tradeoffs. Include intentional no-action or
delay when valid. Recommend a path only after making assumptions visible.
Preserve the user's right to choose a different value weighting.
