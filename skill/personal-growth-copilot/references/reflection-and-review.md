# Reflection and review

## Review purpose

Convert experience into an updated model and next decision. Do not use review
to assign blame, extract confession, or protect the original recommendation.

## Review sequence

1. **Intent:** What did the user plan, and what did they expect?
2. **Actual:** What happened, in observable sequence?
3. **Difference:** Where did reality depart from the plan?
4. **Causes:** Which explanation is supported? Which alternatives remain?
5. **Resources:** What helped, including partial attempts and exceptions?
6. **Learning:** What changed in the working model?
7. **Decision:** continue, modify, pause, stop, or escalate to other support?
8. **Next test:** what single variable should change?

Separate:

- outcome quality from decision quality;
- effort from effect;
- temporary context from durable pattern;
- controllable contribution from uncontrollable circumstance;
- evidence from self-judgment.

## Specific review prompts

- “What happened that someone else could have observed?”
- “What did you predict that did not happen?”
- “What made the action easier or harder than expected?”
- “What is the strongest alternative explanation?”
- “Which part should be repeated exactly?”
- “What should be changed, and what should not?”
- “Does this still serve the goal?”

Avoid “Why didn't you?” as a default. It often collapses event analysis into
defense or self-judgment.

## Progress signals

Use the minimum signal needed to inform the next decision. Possible signals:

- completed behavior or meaningful partial step;
- frequency or duration, if relevant;
- delay between cue and action;
- environmental friction removed;
- skill rehearsal quality;
- user-defined confidence or effort;
- outcome observation;
- boundary respected;
- recovery time after a miss.

Do not optimize the metric while losing the goal. Stop monitoring if it becomes
burdensome, compulsive, shame-inducing, or misleading.

## Decision-quality check

A good outcome can follow a poor decision and a poor outcome can follow a sound
one. Review whether the decision used the information available at the time,
made uncertainty visible, considered alternatives, respected constraints, and
included a reasonable stop condition.

## Closing a review

Write a two- to five-line learning record:

```text
Observed:
Learned:
Updated hypothesis:
Next decision or experiment:
Review event/date:
```

Ask the user to correct it before any persistence.
