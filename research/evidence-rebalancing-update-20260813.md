## Coaching studies: different questions and limited transfer

The [2025 Xie and Ostrowski paper](https://doi.org/10.1080/17439760.2025.2498132)
was reviewed through its [author-uploaded full text](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/391221899_Comparing_the_effectiveness_of_LLM-Powered_coaching_with_human_coaching_and_GPT_conversation).
It randomized 121 people, with 32 completers after one 20-minute session.
Empathy and encouragement comparisons favored the structured AI over generic
GPT; engagement was nonsignificant. Attrition and immediate subjective measures
limit interpretation. No detected AI/human difference establishes equivalence.
The supplement records sample details and the paper's inconsistent criterion
count without selecting a favorable interpretation.

The [2026 De Haan, Terblanche, and Nowack study](https://doi.org/10.1080/13678868.2026.2633990)
reported 114 participants across human, hybrid AI, and waitlist arms. Human
coaching showed broadly stronger improvements in this setting; not every
contrast was significant. The study was not preregistered, had differential
uptake, and contains inconsistent control counts. The [institutional record](https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/a-randomised-controlled-comparison-of-the-effectiveness-of-human-/)
was checked against a [publisher-formatted paper on a public mirror](https://a.storyblok.com/f/45434/x/8a08babd23/research_ai_vs_human_coaching.pdf).
The author's own PDF endpoint was unavailable; a successful author-host download
is not claimed.

These interventions, samples, durations, and outcomes differ. The studies are
not direct replications and do not support a pooled effect estimate for PGC.
They motivate comparison and product-specific delayed observation without
proving human equivalence or universal AI-coaching ineffectiveness. The 2025
entry remains deferred; the 2026 proposed addition informs claim limits, not
runtime efficacy admission.

## Technique naming and longitudinal methods

The [2023 BCTO publication record](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/205776/)
describes 281 techniques in 20 groups for that published version. The eight
registry identifiers and labels were separately verified against the pinned
official ontology recorded in `docs/EVIDENCE_REBALANCING.md`. Naming does not
establish fit, safety, or effectiveness; each candidate retains eligibility,
contraindications, burden, a predicted signal, disconfirmation, alternatives,
and user choice.

[Klasnja et al. (2015)](https://dept.stat.lsa.umich.edu/~tewaria/research/klasnja15microrandomized.pdf)
explain repeated decision-point randomization to estimate proximal component
effects and contextual moderation, in contrast to standard parallel trials of
integrated packages. The citation year is corrected while the stable source ID
`micro-randomized-trials-2016` is retained. Preregistration is PGC's own design
requirement. Any future adaptation requires consent and independent review;
this integration neither randomizes support nor authorizes covert experiments.

[SCRIBE 2016](https://www.equator-network.org/reporting-guidelines/scribe-statement/)
is a reporting guideline for single-case experimental designs. Its
[primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jsp.2016.04.001) and official registry
support reporting completeness, not causal validity of an uncontrolled history.
Borrowing fields for episode-level feasibility reports is a PGC adaptation.
Publisher access was restricted; the review does not claim a complete publisher
PDF read. A short uncontrolled pilot cannot establish general efficacy.

## Governance sources

The [ICF Code of Ethics](https://coachingfederation.org/credentialing/coaching-ethics/icf-code-of-ethics/)
is effective April 1, 2025. The [official AI framework page](https://coachingfederation.org/resource/icf-artificial-intelligence-ai-coaching-framework-and-standards/)
was published November 8, 2024; its [version 1.01 PDF](https://coachingfederation.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/icf-research-ai-coaching-framework-standards.pdf)
is revised October 8, 2024 and includes Basic and Advanced standards across
six domains. [NIST AI 600-1](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.AI.600-1) provides
voluntary generative-AI risk-management guidance. `docs/GOVERNANCE_CROSSWALK.md`
labels PGC-authored mappings and abbreviated domain names. Mapping a control
is not ICF approval, credentialing, NIST certification, or proof it works.

## Evidence still required

Current deterministic qualification covers source structure and controls only.
The 14 research cases and four comparator definitions need frozen execution
contracts, complete attempt preservation, calibrated English/Chinese human
review, and independently authored holdouts before behavioral comparison.
Named-host privacy/security validation and a consented feasibility pilot remain
separate. Outcomes must preserve non-attempts, missing reviews, withdrawals,
burden, and dependence; the current two primary outcome IDs remain fixed.
No target-model, host, pilot, efficacy, installation, or promotion claim is
created by this research integration.
